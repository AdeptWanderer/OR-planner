# Results vs. the paper (paper time budget)

*Final; 13/13 instances; verification suite all green.*

Reference: Hashemi Doulabi, Rousseau & Pesant (2016), *INFORMS J. Computing* 28(3), Table 6
(branch-and-price-and-cut).

## Bottom line

On the paper's own time budget, our implementation of the paper's method averages a **2.37% optimality
gap against the paper's 2.81%**. It proves 6 of 13 instance sizes optimal, against 2 in the paper. Every
schedule is valid, and every reported number re-derives from its saved solver state.

That is not a like-for-like win. Our instances are rebuilt from the paper's description, not the
originals. Two of our set C instances are much easier than the paper's, and B100/B120 duplicate
A100/A120. On the hardest distinct long-case instance (A120) we are clearly **worse** than the paper:
5.70% against 1.40%.

The fair summary: the method behaves as the paper reports. Small and medium instances close to (near)
optimality, and gaps grow with size. Numbers within the same order of magnitude are as much as this
comparison can show.

## Setup (identical to Sec. 6.2 unless noted)

* Column generation 2 h, CP limit 10 s in the fast phase (600 s cap in the optimality phase, where the paper
  uses ∞), branching 2 h with node optimality proven for the first 15 min, final IP 1 h.
* One CPU core per instance, as in the paper. Hardware: a current cloud vCPU, against the paper's 2011 Xeon X5675.
  Equal seconds are therefore **not** equal compute: our core is probably faster, which favours us.
* Solvers: HiGHS + OR-Tools CP-SAT, against the paper's CPLEX + CP Optimizer.
* One instance per size (seed 1), against the paper's average over 5.
* "Paper method" excludes our CP-SAT polishing extension, which is shown in its own column.

## How to read this, and what it does not show

1. **These are not the paper's instances.** We regenerated them from Sec. 6.1, but three inputs had to be
   reconstructed:
   * the surgeon limits, from Fei et al. (2009) Table 1;
   * the room-sizing heuristic for sets B and C (online Appendix 6);
   * the set C Pearson III parameters.

   The magnitudes are close. Our A40 plan is 7,010 min against the paper's 1,403 slots (7,015 min). But no
   per-instance claim of "better" or "worse" is meaningful.
2. **C80 is trivial in our generator.** The heuristic already schedules every case (0% gain, solved at the root).
   The optimal C60 plan also schedules all demand. The paper's C80 was hard (9.92%). Our set C rooms are
   evidently more generous than the paper's, so the set C average flatters us.
3. **B100 and B120 duplicate A100 and A120.** With the same seed our generator draws identical cases for A and B,
   and the room heuristic also yields 6 rooms at those sizes. The "11 distinct instances" row removes the duplicates.
4. **One instance per size**, against five in the paper, so single rows carry more noise than the paper's averages.
5. **Where we are clearly behind:**
   * A120: 5.70% against 1.40%.
   * C100 with the paper's method: 15.68% against 12.01%.
   * C60 with the paper's method: 3.36% against 0.94%.
   * B40 needed the full 2 h branching budget for 0.22%, where the paper closed its B40 in 41 min.

   On long-case instances at our largest size the primal side (finding the integer plan) is the weak point.
   On short-case instances, combining time-indexed columns into an integer plan is hard. The paper reports
   the same difficulty (its set C gaps are its largest), and our polishing extension addresses it.
6. **Compute is not equal.** Same wall-clock seconds and one core each, but different CPU generations and
   different solvers.

## Fidelity: is this the paper's method?

See [`VERIFICATION.md`](../VERIFICATION.md) for the element-by-element traceability matrix. In short:

* **Faithful:**
  * problem definition and assumptions;
  * master (1)–(7);
  * reduced cost;
  * DR1, DR2 and DR4 as stated;
  * fast infeasibility detection (steps 1–5);
  * Algorithms 1 and 2;
  * all three branching rules in order;
  * the cut and branching duals in pricing;
  * the time budget.
* **Adapted, with equivalence tested:**
  * CP-SAT interval model instead of the position model. Same optimum as the literal position model on every
    tested dual vector.
  * Dominance rules and the knapsack check applied at the root of each CP search instead of dynamically.
  * Conservative λ in DR3.
  * Greedy cover separation.
  * Lifting corrected: eq. (39) as printed is invalid (counterexample in the tests).
  * Artificial variables in the master.
* **Extensions, kept out of the paper-method numbers:**
  * Lagrangian upper bound;
  * redundant makespan constraint;
  * CP-SAT polishing;
  * checkpoint/resume.

**Findings the verification suite produced:**

1. **DR1 is not an exact pruning rule for pricing.** For some dual vectors the best column starts at slot 1…SD−1,
   and DR1 removes it. The paper's proof establishes that an optimal *integer* solution satisfies DR1, so the
   rule is a valid restriction and bounds stay valid. Our tests now verify DR2–DR4 and the knapsack check as
   exact on top of DR1.
2. **Eq. (39) as printed yields invalid cuts.** We use the standard Balas form.
3. Earlier in development: **a node-pruning bug**. A node was declared infeasible when artificials were positive
   at convergence, which made a reported 0% gap invalid. It was found by exactness checks, fixed, and all
   benchmark numbers above were produced after the fix.
4. **Untested paths in the first test set.** The knapsack infeasibility path and DR4 had never fired. New
   cases now exercise both.

**Verification suite (final run):**
* 13/13 audited instances pass;
* 99/99 pricing-conformance tests;
* 27/27 model, cut, branching and algorithm tests;
* 18/18 exactness tests against two independent exact solvers;
* 10/10 unit tests.

Details are in `VERIFICATION.md` §8.

**Results provenance.** `verification/audit_results.py` regenerated all 13 instances and re-validated each
stored schedule. It recomputed every reported plan, heuristic value and gap, checked bound consistency and
time limits, and cross-checked each upper bound against an independent CP-SAT model: **13/13 pass all 13
checks.**
